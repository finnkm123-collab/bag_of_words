Pythagoras' Theorem

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse
(the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the
squares of the other two sides.

This relationship can be expressed as:

    a^2 + b^2 = c^2

where a and b are the lengths of the two legs, and c is the
length of the hypotenuse.

The theorem is one of the fundamental results in Euclidean geometry,
with applications ranging from architecture to physics and
modern computer science.
